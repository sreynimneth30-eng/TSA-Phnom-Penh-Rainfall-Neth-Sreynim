 1. What I learned

I also learned how to create a time plot to see how precipitation changes over the 11-year period. A seasonal plot helped me compare rainfall patterns between different years.

Another important thing I learned was the seasonal subseries plot. It allows me to compare the same month across different years and see how much the values vary.

 2. What I found from the data

The data contains 11 years of rainfall data from 2015 to 2025, with 12 months for each year.

From the monthly averages:

* September has the highest average precipitation: 227.46
* February has the lowest average precipitation: 12.19
* The difference between the highest and lowest monthly averages is 215.27
* The largest individual monthly value is 703.62, occurring in June 2020

The large value does not automatically mean that it is an error because rainfall can naturally vary from month to month and year to year.

 3. What I learned about evidence

I learned that when making a claim from a graph, I should support it with a number or something clearly visible in the plot.

For example, instead of only saying that September has high rainfall, I can use the monthly mean of 227.46 as evidence.

I also learned that a seasonal pattern does not mean every year must have exactly the same rainfall.
